Keep first-touch attribution for a year. The browser now sends its first touch to `POST /_agent-native/auth/attribution`, which keeps it in an `an_fts` cookie that our server sets, expiring 365 days after the first visit. That copy survives Safari's seven-day limit on cookies written by page JavaScript, restores the browser's own copies when they are cleared, and is the first touch signup reads unless the browser holds a better one. Last touch still lasts 30 days, and the server now writes `an_ft` and `an_lt` again so Safari keeps them for those 30 days. Visitors in the EU, the rest of the EEA and a few nearby territories (the countries builder.io asks for consent) get no attribution stored until they opt in to targeting cookies. The opt-in lives in an `an_consent` cookie, written by the new `@agent-native/core/client/consent` module and again by our server (`POST /_agent-native/auth/consent`) so Safari keeps it for its full year, which also tells the app whether this visitor is in that region (`GET /_agent-native/auth/consent-region`, from the request's country, with the time zone as fallback). A later reject clears stored attribution. Everyone else is tracked as before.
