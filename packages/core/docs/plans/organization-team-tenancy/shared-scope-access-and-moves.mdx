# Shared scope, access, and moves

Parent: [organization-team tenancy implementation plan](./plan.mdx)

## Decision

Resource scope is a durable tenancy attribute, not ownership or a replacement for the sharing ACL. A team resource is one organization-owned resource associated with one team, not a duplicate resource. Its human owner remains distinct from organization ownership. Team association alone grants no organization-wide read. Visibility and grants determine access. The shared layer gains a first-class `team` principal and a fail-closed scope resolver. Scope moves are authorized, audited mutations that preserve the human owner and reject incompatible shares.

## Grounded seams

- `packages/core/src/sharing/schema.ts` defines reusable share-table shapes.
- `packages/core/src/sharing/access.ts` exports `accessFilter` and is the shared read-access boundary.
- `packages/core/src/sharing/registry.ts` is the resource-family registration seam.
- `packages/core/src/resources/handlers.ts` is the baseline resource HTTP-handler seam named by the ADR.

## Ordered steps

1. Define a shared scope contract for `personal`, `organization`, and `team`, including required `orgId`/`teamId` combinations and a server-derived membership check. Keep the contract close to `sharing/access.ts` and registration metadata rather than duplicating it per handler.
2. Add the `team` share principal in `sharing/schema.ts` and extend `accessFilter` in `sharing/access.ts` to combine owner, explicit grant, organization visibility, organization administration, and current team membership without changing legacy personal-resource ACLs. Require verified current organization membership for organization-derived list and direct authorization, including organization-visible resources and organization-principal shares. Use a SQL-compatible membership predicate or verified context, not per-row lookups. Preserve explicit user shares.
3. Register only completed resource families in `sharing/registry.ts`. A disabled or unregistered family denies team scope and team-principal grants; malformed, unknown, or mismatched rows never fall back to personal or organization behavior.
4. Design the explicit resource-scope move mutation at the shared boundary only for authored families that declare move support, including linked-data and share effects. Exclude credentials, history/runs, team-bound context, and other operational records. Authorize personal-to-shared moves with target create access; require owner/admin authority for moves out of or between shared scopes; prohibit cross-organization and invalid team-bound moves; validate all retained shares before committing and write audit history.
5. Update `resources/handlers.ts` and focused family call sites only after the shared contract exists. Apply the same fail-closed team-scope validation in direct `resolveAccess` before owner, admin, public, organization, or share branches and before `assertAccess`. Include scope fields in projected access loads. End team-derived access when organization membership becomes inactive, including `federationRemovalPendingAt`, before team rows are cleaned up. Inventory direct item, list, search, export, and mutation paths before registering a family.
6. Add focused tests for legacy personal rows with an `orgId`, organization visibility, team membership, direct cross-team grants, organization-admin override, direct `resolveAccess` validation before every authorization branch, inactive and `federationRemovalPendingAt` membership, unknown scope, disabled family scope, incompatible shares, and audited move denials.

## Acceptance criteria

- Scope and human owner remain distinct, and supported scope moves preserve the owner.
- A team-scoped resource is one organization-owned resource associated with that team. The association alone grants no organization-wide read.
- Team removal revokes only team-derived access; direct compatible shares continue to work.
- Organization visibility remains additive and cannot be narrowed by a team grant.
- Organization-derived list and direct access require verified current organization membership. Explicit user shares remain valid independently.
- Direct `resolveAccess` and `assertAccess` validate team scope fail-closed before every early authorization branch and project the required scope fields. Team-derived access is denied immediately when organization membership is inactive or has `federationRemovalPendingAt`, even if its team-membership row awaits cleanup.
- No access or create/move path coerces unknown, mismatched, disabled, or partial-migration team scope into another scope.
- A scope move is auditable and rejects, rather than drops, incompatible existing shares.
- No generic move exists for a family that did not explicitly declare its support and linked-data/share behavior.

## Risks and non-goals

- Do not grant team leads resource administration merely because they manage membership.
- Do not use active-team selection as an input to `accessFilter` authority.
- Do not enable a family by adding columns alone; family readiness belongs to [Content family rollout and readiness](./content-family-rollout-and-readiness.mdx).
