# Organization-team tenancy implementation plan

## Outcome

Implement the proposed [organization-team tenancy ADR](../../design/organization-team-tenancy.md): users retain one active organization selection while belonging to multiple organizations; teams belong to one organization; team-scoped resources are organization-owned with separate human owners; and access validates scope before grants. The implementation introduces opt-in teams inside an organization and lets a resource family opt into a complete personal, organization, and team tenancy contract. A selected team is a validated filter and default placement, never an authorization grant.

## Hard decisions

- Scope is durable and distinct from the human owner and existing visibility/grants. Every enabled resource has exactly one `personal`, `organization`, or `team` scope.
- Schema evolution is additive. Teams change no pre-existing row's owner, scope or effective legacy scope, `orgId` association, visibility, ACL, or behavior. `orgId` alone is not organization ownership.
- Unknown, mismatched, or disabled team scope fails closed. Each family flag remains off until its migration, complete inventory, trusted enforcement, and local checks are complete. No deployment gate is required.
- Scope moves are explicit, audited operations that preserve the human owner and reject incompatible shares. Generic Core moves are only for authored families that declare move support and linked-data/share effects.
- Teams have explicit governance: organization owners/admins govern all teams, including leadless teams. Leads manage only their team membership and have no resource or credential authority. Deletion is archival.
- Organization-owned connections are separately granted. Durable runs, jobs, and automations persist scope context but revalidate current authority before resume and each external effect.

## Rollout gates

1. Keep every family disabled by default, including Content.
2. Before enabling a family, complete an inventory of its read, list, search, export, item, mutation, sharing, HTTP, background, agent, and automation paths.
3. Require the additive migration to preserve every pre-existing row's effective behavior. No implicit mapping or reclassification is allowed.
4. Complete Core first. Then migrate and locally prove every template separately before enabling its family flag.
5. Do not require deployment, live-server compatibility, or readiness proof to complete the work.

## Deferred and out of scope

- Multi-team ownership, team-owned credentials, deny/hide inheritance semantics, and organization-admin authority narrowed below the organization.
- Enabling other resource families, broad backfills, implicit scope conversion, and team-scoped ownership inferred from a share.
- Only rewriting the ADR into a task tracker or changing its proposed status.

## Verification strategy

Each child plan defines focused acceptance tests. At the suite level, verify the three scopes and current membership/authority at direct access, list/search/export, explicit share, supported move, connection use, run resume, automation dispatch, selection/deep-link, offboarding, and archival boundaries. Exercise pre-existing rows, organization inheritance, direct cross-team shares, revoked membership/grants, unknown scope, and disabled family scope. Locally prove each template family. Content also proves transclusion, traversal, embeds, projections, caches, snippets, counts, pagination, search, export, AI, and background surfaces.
