A sales presentation is the deck you walk one prospect through after the discovery call. It covers their problem, how you solve it, proof from a customer like them, and the next step. To make a sales presentation that moves the deal, build it from what the prospect told you in discovery. This guide takes you from your discovery notes to a deck you can present and leave behind.

## What you need

- Discovery-call notes or a recording
- The deal in your CRM
- Your team's standard deck
- Two or three customer stories you can share

## How to make a sales presentation

### 1. Decide the next step the meeting should reach

A sales presentation exists to move the deal one step, so **write that step down before you make a slide**. Make it something the prospect can agree to in the room: a pilot, a meeting with their finance lead, a proposal by a date. Write it as one sentence, such as "By the end of this meeting, Pinecrest agrees to a 30-day pilot at two hotels."

Then list who's attending and what each person decides. The **economic buyer**, the person who signs, wants cost and risk. Your champion, the contact who wants the change, needs a deck they can defend inside their company. The people who'll use the product want to know how their day changes.

### 2. Collect what the prospect told you in discovery

Everything the deck says about the prospect comes from discovery, so **gather it in their words** on one page:

| What to capture | Where it usually lives |
|---|---|
| The problem, in their words | Your call notes or the call recording |
| What the problem costs them | Call notes, follow-up emails |
| What they've tried already | Call notes |
| Who decides and who else is involved | The deal in your CRM |
| Their deadline, or what made them look now | Call notes, the CRM deal |
| Questions or doubts they raised | Call notes or the call recording |

With deals in HubSpot and calls in Gong, you can [ask Agent-Native Analytics about one deal and its calls](https://www.agent-native.com/use-cases/ai-sales-analytics/). Copy exact quotes: a slide in their own words lands better than your paraphrase. Priya, Pinecrest's finance director, said invoices get approved by forwarded email across 14 hotels, which became slide 2. Plan to ask about anything missing early in the meeting.

### 3. Write the story as slide headlines

Turn the discovery page into **one headline per slide, starting from the prospect's situation**. A good order is their situation, what it costs them, one headline per problem, then why you over the alternative. Aim for 6–10 headlines, counting the title and the next step. For the Pinecrest example, the first headlines could be "Invoices wait in 14 inboxes before anyone approves them" and "Late approvals cost Pinecrest early-payment discounts."

Give your company one slide at most. The headlines alone should read as the story of the meeting, the same test consultants use when they [write every slide title before building a consulting deck](https://www.agent-native.com/articles/consulting-deck/).

### 4. Match each problem to what you sell

For each problem the prospect named, write **what solves it and how you'll show it**. Leave out any feature that doesn't answer a problem on the list, however good it is. Here's the Pinecrest example:

| Their problem | What solves it | How you'll show it |
|---|---|---|
| Invoices sit in email | One approval queue for all 14 hotels | A screenshot of the queue |
| Nobody knows who's holding an invoice | Each invoice shows its approver and how long it has waited | A 2-minute live demo |
| Early-payment discounts are missed | Reminders before the discount date | A result from a similar customer |

The table becomes the middle of the deck, with one slide per row. Then write one line on why you over what they use today, from "what they've tried already" in your discovery page. For Pinecrest, that's email and their accounting system's approval step.

### 5. Pick proof from a customer like them

Proof works when the prospect sees themselves in it, so **choose the customer closest to them** in industry, size and problem over the biggest name you have. Put one piece of proof per problem in the table's last column. It can be a result with its number and source, a short quote, or a live demo when seeing it beats a slide.

Check that each customer has agreed to be named. If they haven't, describe them instead: "a 20-hotel group in the Midwest".

### 6. Build the slides from an outline

Give each slide **one headline and the proof behind it**, using this outline. The prospect slides are new, and the rest come from your standard deck.

| Slide | Built from |
|---|---|
| Title, with the proposed next step | Your next-step sentence |
| Their situation, in their words | Discovery quotes |
| What it costs them | The cost they described |
| One slide per problem | Each row of the problem table, with its proof |
| Why you over the alternative | Your why-you line |
| How it works | The standard deck, or a short demo |
| Investment, if the deal is at that stage | Your pricing for their size |
| Next step, owners and dates | Your next-step sentence |
| Backup slides | Answers to expected objections, with standard deck slides |

Keep the main deck to about 10 slides, leaving half the meeting for talk. To go faster next time, **copy this deck for each prospect** and change only the prospect slides.

### 7. Prepare for the objections

List the doubts the prospect raised in discovery, then add the common ones: price, timing, the effort to switch, and "we already have something for this". **Write a short answer and add a backup slide for each**, after the main deck. For Pinecrest, "our accounting system already has approvals" gets a backup slide showing that approved invoices still go to it to be paid.

### 8. Present it as a conversation

Rehearse the deck once with a teammate who plays the economic buyer. In the meeting, open with their situation slide and **ask whether it's still right** before you go further, because things change between discovery and the meeting. Ask a question on each problem slide, and skip slides the prospect doesn't care about. End on the next-step slide and ask for that step directly.

On a video call, share one slide at a time in full screen, and send the deck link before the call so people who join late can follow.

### 9. Follow up with the deck and next steps

**Send a short recap the same day**: what you agreed, each next step with its owner and date, and the deck. Make that deck work without you: put your spoken points in the slides or the speaker notes. Your champion will forward it to people who weren't in the room.

For Pinecrest, the recap names the two pilot hotels Priya, the finance director, picks by Nov 14 and the review date, Dec 19. When the deal closes, you can reuse the same discovery notes to start the customer's first [quarterly business review (QBR) presentation](https://www.agent-native.com/articles/qbr-presentation/).

## Common problems

### You have no discovery notes

You may get a meeting without a proper discovery call. Keep the problem slides short and general, and open the meeting with two or three discovery questions. Then skip to the slides that match what the prospect says.

### The buyer who decides doesn't come

The next step usually needs the person who signs. Keep the meeting going with the people who came, and change the ask to a short meeting with the buyer. Send your recap and the leave-behind deck so your contact can make the case internally.

### The prospect asks about pricing on slide 2

Answer with a range or how you price, then say you'll show the full investment once you've agreed on the problems. Keep an investment slide in the backup slides for prospects who won't wait.

### The meeting turns into a demo

A prospect who asks to see the product is interested, so show it. Tie each part of the demo to a problem from the table, and come back to the next-step slide before time runs out.

## Questions

### What slides go in a sales presentation?

A sales presentation needs the prospect's situation, what it costs them, one slide per problem with proof, why you over the alternative, and a clear next step. Everything else is optional. Company history, team photos and full feature lists are the slides to cut first, because they're about you rather than the buyer.

### How long should a sales presentation be?

Plan for half the meeting at most. In a 30-minute meeting, that's about 15 minutes of slides and 15 of conversation, which fits a main deck of 6–10 slides plus backups. The prospect's questions matter more than finishing the deck.

### What's the difference between a sales presentation and a pitch deck?

A sales presentation sells a product to a customer and is built for one prospect, from what they told you. A [pitch deck](https://www.agent-native.com/use-cases/ai-pitch-deck-maker/) usually asks investors for funding and covers the company: the problem, the market, the business model and the team. A pitch deck changes little from one investor to the next.

### What should you send after a sales presentation?

Send a recap and the deck the same day, while the meeting is fresh. If you promised something in the meeting, such as a security document or a price, send it with the recap or give a date for it. A read-only link instead of an attachment means the prospect always sees your latest version.

### How can you make a sales presentation faster?

Keep one base deck and copy it for each prospect, so only the title, situation, cost and problem slides change. Keep your discovery notes in the same shape every time, so each copy fills in from the same headings. An AI slide tool such as Agent-Native Slides can build those prospect slides from your notes, but check every quote and number it uses.

### What are the 5 steps of a sales presentation?

The steps differ between sales methods. A common version is: prepare from discovery, open with the prospect's situation, show how you solve each problem, handle objections, and ask for the next step. This guide splits preparation into smaller steps, because that's where most of the work is.
