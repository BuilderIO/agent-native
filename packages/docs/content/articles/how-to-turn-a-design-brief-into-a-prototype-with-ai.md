Write a short brief, ask an AI design tool for a few directions, and refine the one you pick. The AI drafts the screens. Your job is the brief and the choices. A vague brief gets vague prototypes, so most of the work happens before you ask for anything.

## What does the AI need from your brief?

| Put in the brief       | Why the AI needs it                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The goal               | It decides what the screen has to get someone to do.                          |
| The audience           | It shapes the language, the density, and what to explain.                     |
| Scope and deliverables | It tells the AI which screens and states to draw, and which to skip.          |
| Brand and style        | It keeps the draft on brand instead of generic.                               |
| Constraints            | It rules out directions you can't ship, like a deadline or a technical limit. |

## What's a brief template you can paste into an AI tool?

```
Project:
Overview (2-3 sentences):

Goal (one outcome):

Audience:
- Who they are:
- What they already know:

Scope and deliverables:
- In:
- Out:

Brand and style:
- Colors and type:
- Tone:
- Avoid:

Constraints:
- Deadline:
- Technical limits:
- Required content:
```

## How does it work in Design?

1. Paste the brief into Design. Link a design system if you have one, so colors, type, and styling carry through.
2. Ask for a few directions. Design shows them side by side on the canvas.
3. Refine the one you like with visual controls, or ask the agent to change it. Pin comments to specific elements.
4. Export HTML or a ZIP of the files, or prepare a handoff for a coding agent.

## What does a good brief look like?

Here's a short, made-up example for a landing page.

> Project: Landing page for a habit-tracking app.
> Goal: Waitlist signups.
> Audience: People who've tried habit apps and quit after a few weeks.
> Scope: Hero, three features, FAQ, signup form. No pricing page.
> Brand: Calm, plain language, one accent color.
> Constraints: Needs to ship Friday. Static HTML only.

## What should you check before you use it?

Check the brand rules against what comes back. The prototype is a starting point, not a finished app: application logic, integrations, testing, and deployment still need implementation and review. Design is labeled alpha, so expect rough edges.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI turn a design brief into a prototype?

Yes. AI design tools can draft interactive prototypes from a brief. Design creates interactive HTML prototypes that you can compare side by side and refine.

### How long should a design brief be?

One page is usually enough. If it runs longer, the extra detail often belongs in a separate spec.

### Can I use my own design system?

Yes. Link a design system to guide colors, typography, styling, and brand instructions. You can reuse it across designs.

### Is the prototype a finished app?

No. It's a starting point for development. Application logic, integrations, testing, and deployment still need implementation and review.

### Is Design free?

Yes. Design is free and open source. It's labeled alpha, so expect rough edges.
