Meeting minutes are a group's approved record of a meeting: who attended, each decision, each motion and its result, and each action item with an owner and a date. To write them, build a template from the agenda, fill in the outcome of each item during the meeting, and write them up the same day. The chair checks them, and the group approves them at its next meeting. This guide walks through each step, with a full example from a made-up nonprofit board.

## What you need

- The meeting's agenda
- The list of members and guests
- The group's quorum rule, if it votes
- Last meeting's minutes and open action items
- Permission to record, under your group's rules

## How to write meeting minutes

### 1. Build a template from the agenda

**Copy the agenda into a document and add slots under each item**, so during the meeting you only write outcomes. Ask the chair or the last secretary for past minutes, and match their layout. Here's the template Tom Becker, secretary of the Riverside Community Garden board, filled in before the October meeting:

| Section | What goes in it | Riverside, before the meeting |
|---|---|---|
| Header | Group, type of meeting, date, start time, place or call link | Riverside Community Garden, board, regular meeting, October 13, 2026, 7:00 p.m., Zoom |
| Attendance | Present, absent, guests, chair, minute-taker, quorum | All seven members listed, to mark on the night |
| Last minutes | Approved as sent, or with corrections | Minutes of September 8 |
| One block per agenda item | Outcome, motion, action items | Treasurer's report · Plot fees for 2027 · Tool shed repair |
| Close | Next meeting, time adjourned | November 10, 2026 |

### 2. Record who's there

Attendance shows who made the decisions. **Mark each member present or absent**, and list guests by name and role. Note who chaired and who took the minutes.

At Riverside, that line reads: "Present: six members, Maria Chen in the chair. Absent: Jo Allen."

If the group votes, count the members present against its quorum rule, the smallest number who must attend for a vote to count. Write "A quorum was present" in the minutes. When someone arrives late or leaves early, note the time, because the count for later votes changes.

### 3. Write the outcome of each item

Minutes record what the group did: its decisions, reports and plans. For each agenda item, **write the outcome in one or two sentences**: what was decided, reported or put off. Add the main points of the discussion only if your group expects them, and leave names out of the discussion.

Here's one item from the Riverside Community Garden board, as raw notes and as minutes:

| Raw notes | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Dana: raise plot fee to $45? Sam worried about members on low incomes. Long back and forth. Hardship fund? Sam to look into it. | **Plot fees for 2027.** The board discussed raising the annual plot fee and its effect on members with low incomes. The board agreed to raise the fee and asked Sam Okafor to look into a hardship fund. |

The minutes keep the decision and the reason, and drop the back and forth.

### 4. Write motions word for word

A motion is a formal proposal the group votes on, and its wording is the decision. **Write each motion exactly as it was put to the vote**, then who moved it, who seconded it and the result. A clear motion names the action, any amount, and when it takes effect.

If you didn't catch the wording, ask the chair to repeat it before the vote.

In the Riverside Community Garden example, the motion reads: "To raise the annual plot fee from $40 to $45 for 2027. Moved by Dana Ruiz, seconded by Lee Park. Carried, 5 in favor, 1 against."

If the group counts votes, record the count, and list abstentions separately from votes against. Name voters only when someone asks for their vote to be recorded.

Board meetings add two things to note. When a member declares a conflict of interest, record it and whether they left for the vote. When the board meets in closed session, record the time and the general reason, and leave the discussion out.

### 5. Log action items with owners and dates

**Give every task one owner and a due date**, using a person's name instead of "the committee", so everyone knows who to ask about it. Write each one as a short line: who does what, by when. At Riverside: "Sam Okafor to draft a hardship fund proposal by November 6."

Before the meeting ends, read the action items back. People will correct an owner or a date then, instead of after the minutes go out.

### 6. Write up the minutes the same day

Your memory of the meeting fades fast, so **write the minutes up the same day**, or the next morning at the latest. Turn your notes into full sentences:

- Use the past tense and the third person: "The board approved…".
- Keep them neutral: no adjectives about the debate and no opinions.
- Check every name, amount and date.
- Check motion wording against the recording or the chair's notes, if you have either.

Here are the finished minutes for the Riverside Community Garden example:

Riverside Community Garden, board of directors, regular meeting. Tuesday, October 13, 2026, 7:00 p.m., on Zoom.

- **Present:** Maria Chen (chair), Dana Ruiz (treasurer), Lee Park, Sam Okafor, Priya Nair, Tom Becker (secretary). Absent: Jo Allen. A quorum was present.
- **Call to order:** The chair called the meeting to order at 7:02 p.m.
- **Minutes:** The minutes of September 8, 2026 were approved as sent.
- **Treasurer's report:** Dana Ruiz reported a balance of $8,240 on September 30. The report was filed.
- **Plot fees for 2027:** The board discussed raising the annual plot fee and its effect on members with low incomes. Motion: To raise the annual plot fee from $40 to $45 for 2027. Moved by Dana Ruiz, seconded by Lee Park. Carried, 5 in favor, 1 against. Sam Okafor will look into a hardship fund for members with low incomes.
- **Tool shed repair:** Lee Park declared a conflict of interest and left the meeting for this item. Motion: To accept Northside Builders' quote of $1,900 to repair the tool shed roof. Moved by Priya Nair, seconded by Sam Okafor. Carried, 5 in favor, none against.
- **Action items:** Sam Okafor to draft a hardship fund proposal by November 6. Dana Ruiz to update the fee on the sign-up form by November 1. Tom Becker to send these minutes by October 14.
- **Next meeting:** November 10, 2026, 7:00 p.m.
- **Adjourned:** 8:14 p.m.

Minutes submitted by Tom Becker, secretary. Draft, to be approved on November 10.

### 7. Have the chair check the draft

The chair ran the meeting, so the chair is the best check on what was decided. **Send the written-up minutes to the chair first**, and fix what they correct. Then send them to every member, including those who were absent, with "Draft" in the title until the group approves them. Tom's reads "Riverside board minutes, October 13, 2026 (draft)".

### 8. Get the minutes approved and file them

Minutes become the official record only when the group approves them. Put **approval of the last minutes** near the top of the next agenda. Members can propose corrections, which you make before the vote to approve.

The next meeting's minutes record the vote. At Riverside, that's "The minutes of October 13 were approved as sent."

The chair or the secretary then signs the approved version, if your group signs minutes.

File the approved minutes with the agenda and any reports handed out, where the group keeps its records. Keep the draft and the approved version apart, so nobody quotes the wrong one.

## Common problems

### You missed what was decided

The discussion moved on before you wrote the outcome down. Ask the chair to state the decision before moving to the next item: "So the decision is…". If the meeting has ended, check the recording, or ask the chair before you send the draft.

### The discussion wandered off the agenda

When a topic comes up that isn't on the agenda, record it under "Other business" with its outcome, if there is one. If nothing was decided, leave it out, or note that it was raised and put on the next agenda.

### Nobody will take an action item

Write the item down with the owner left blank, and ask the chair to assign it before the meeting closes. If it's still unassigned, list it as an open item on the next agenda.

### Someone asks to change approved minutes

Approved minutes are the group's record, so you don't edit them quietly. A member can propose a correction at a later meeting. If the group agrees, the correction goes in that meeting's minutes, and you note it on the original.

## Questions

### What should meeting minutes include?

Meeting minutes include the group's name, the date, time and place, who attended and who was absent, and whether there was a quorum. Then, for each agenda item, the outcome, any motion with its mover, seconder and result, and any action item with an owner and a due date. They close with the next meeting's date and the time the meeting ended.

### How detailed should meeting minutes be?

Minutes should be detailed enough that someone who missed the meeting knows what was decided and who's doing what. That usually means one or two sentences per agenda item, plus motions word for word. Leave out who said what, unless your group's rules ask for it.

### What's the difference between meeting minutes and meeting notes?

Meeting minutes are a formal record of decisions that the whole group approves. Meeting notes are informal, written for yourself or your team, and can hold anything useful; see [how to take meeting notes](https://www.agent-native.com/articles/meeting-notes/). Minutes follow a fixed format and stay neutral. A meeting summary is different again: a few lines sent to everyone after a call, covered in [how to write a meeting summary](https://www.agent-native.com/articles/meeting-summary/).

### Do meeting minutes need to be signed?

Whether minutes need a signature depends on your bylaws or the group's own rules. Where minutes are signed, the secretary, the chair or both sign the approved version after the group votes to approve it. The signature shows which copy is the official record.

### Can AI take meeting minutes?

AI can record and transcribe a meeting, then draft minutes from the transcript. Agent-Native Clips does this from your own computer, with nothing joining the call. You still need to check each motion, vote and owner against the transcript, and the group still approves the minutes.

### How are board meeting minutes different?

Board meeting minutes are more formal than team minutes, because they're the board's official record of its decisions, and legal rules for them vary by place and type of organization. They record quorum, each motion word for word with its result, conflicts of interest and closed sessions. The board approves them at its next meeting, and the secretary or chair usually signs them. Check your bylaws for anything else they must include.
