AI can turn your brief and notes into a first draft of the deck, in your brand. What it can't do is decide your story or vouch for your numbers. Give it a clear brief, then check every slide.

## What should you give the AI?

- A short brief: what you do, who it's for, and why now.
- Your real numbers: traction, revenue, and how much you're raising.
- Your brand: colors, fonts, and logo.
- Last round's deck or your notes, if you have them.

The draft can only be as accurate as what you hand it.

## Which slides should the draft include?

There isn't one correct order, but most good decks cover the same ground in about ten slides. Use this list to check the draft:

1. Title, with a one-line description of what you do.
2. The problem, and who has it.
3. Your solution.
4. The product: how it works, ideally with a real screenshot.
5. The market you're going after.
6. Your business model: how you make money.
7. Traction: what's working, with numbers you can stand behind.
8. Competition: what people use today, and why they'd switch.
9. The team, and why you're the ones to build this.
10. The ask: how much you're raising and what it's for.

## What should you check before you send it?

Check every number and every claim. The AI drafts from what you give it, and investors will check too. If you export to PowerPoint, open the file once first, since fonts and layouts can render differently between editors. Slides is labeled alpha, so expect rough edges.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI make a pitch deck?

Yes. AI can draft a deck from your brief and notes. You still own the story and check every number before you send it.

### How many slides should a pitch deck have?

Around ten is a common target. Fewer, clearer slides usually beat more.

### What's the difference between a pitch deck and an investor update?

A pitch deck makes the case for a new investment. An investor update reports progress to people who already invested.

### Can I export a pitch deck to PowerPoint?

Yes. Export a PPTX file to open in PowerPoint. To use it in Google Slides, import that file there. Review fonts and layouts after export, because they can render differently between editors.

### Is Slides free?

Yes. Slides is free and open source. It's labeled alpha, so expect rough edges.
