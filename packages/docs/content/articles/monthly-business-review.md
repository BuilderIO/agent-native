A monthly business review (MBR) is a recurring meeting where a team compares the month with its plan and with last month. The team explains the gaps and agrees what to change. Because it repeats, set up the scorecard and the deck once; each month after that changes only the numbers, the reasons and the actions. This guide covers the scorecard, the deck and the meeting, with an example team.

They show today's decisions, the scorecard against plan and September, one off-plan metric, and the action log.](https://cdn.builder.io/api/v1/image/assets%2FYJIGb4i01jvw0SRdL5Bt%2F5f0cf6d99ff04e7ebb640636390fed20)

## What you need

- The year's plan or monthly targets
- Access to the tools your numbers live in
- The list of attendees
- Last month's deck, after your first MBR

## How to run a monthly business review

### 1. Set up the scorecard once

The scorecard is the fixed list of metrics the MBR reviews every month. **Pick 6 to 10 metrics that measure the plan**, each with a definition, source, owner and off-plan threshold; the monthly targets come from the plan. Pair results like revenue with metrics the team moves directly, here orders and on-time delivery. Brambleroot, a made-up online plant shop, uses this one:

| Metric | Definition | Source | Owner | Off plan when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Net of refunds | Store platform | Finance | 5% below plan |
| Orders | Paid orders | Store platform | Ecommerce | 5% below plan |
| Average order value | Revenue ÷ orders | Store platform | Ecommerce | 5% below plan |
| Repeat customer rate | Orders from returning buyers | Store platform | Marketing | 3 points below plan |
| Cost per new customer | Ad spend ÷ first-time buyers | Ad accounts | Marketing | 5% above plan |
| On-time delivery | Delivered by the promised date | Shipping tool | Operations | 3 points below plan |

Also set three dates each month: numbers day (say, the third working day after close), pre-read day (two working days before the meeting) and the meeting.

### 2. Pull the month's numbers on the same day

The numbers come from the scorecard's sources, pulled once the month has closed. **Pull every metric on numbers day each month**, with the definitions from the scorecard, into one sheet with a column per month. Each owner checks their own number before it goes in.

If your numbers live in Stripe, HubSpot or Google Analytics, you can [ask Agent-Native Analytics for them in plain English](https://www.agent-native.com/use-cases/ai-kpi-dashboard/). The sheet now holds this month's numbers next to every month before it.

### 3. Compare each number with plan and last month

Plan shows whether the team is on track, and last month shows the direction. Put **actual, plan, gap and last month** side by side for every metric. Mark each one on plan or off plan by its threshold; for a cost metric, a negative gap is good. When your business is seasonal, add the same month last year; here's Brambleroot's October:

| Metric | Plan | Actual | Gap | September | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $410,000 | $372,000 | −9% | $395,000 | Off plan |
| Orders | 6,200 | 6,100 | −2% | 6,080 | On plan |
| Average order value | $66 | $61 | −8% | $65 | Off plan |
| Repeat customer rate | 34% | 35% | +1 pt | 33% | On plan |
| Cost per new customer | $38 | $36 | −5% | $39 | On plan |
| On-time delivery | 95% | 88% | −7 pts | 94% | Off plan |

Three of six metrics are off plan, and those three are the meeting.

### 4. Explain only what's off plan

Commentary is for the metrics that missed their threshold. Each metric's owner writes **one line per off-plan reason** before pre-read day: what happened, why, and what changes. Metrics that missed for the same reason share one line. The line becomes a headline that says what the number means, and the headline becomes the slide title.

For Brambleroot, revenue and average order value missed for the same reason: a fall discount on small plants pulled order value down while orders held. The headline is "Revenue missed plan by 9% as the fall discount cut order value". On-time delivery dropped after the carrier moved its pickup time, so its headline is "Late deliveries rose to 12% after the carrier's pickup change".

Metrics on plan get no commentary. Their numbers stay on the scorecard.

### 5. Update last month's actions

Last month's actions come before anything new, so the team sees whether what it agreed happened. **Mark each open action done, late or dropped**, and give every late one a new date. Key projects from the plan get the same three statuses, with their next milestone. Brambleroot's September actions and its one key project:

- Launch the gift card page: done
- Test a second carrier in two regions: late, now due November 15
- Cut the plant-care email to every other week: dropped, because open rates held
- Holiday gift bundles (key project): late, next milestone is the bundle page on November 8

An action that's late two months in a row goes on the agenda as a decision: give it more help, or drop it.

### 6. Build this month's deck from last month's

The deck's order stays the same every month, so **start from a copy of last month's deck** and change only what's new. On the first MBR, build the deck once from this outline:

| Slide | Each month |
|---|---|
| Decisions needed today | Rewritten |
| Last month's actions and key projects | Statuses updated |
| Scorecard: actual, plan, gap, last month | Numbers updated |
| One slide per off-plan reason, headline first | Replaced |
| Trends: each metric over 12 months | One new month added |
| Risks and asks, including budget and hiring | Rewritten |
| New actions, owners and dates | Filled in during the meeting |
| Appendix: definitions and sources | Unchanged |

Show trends as line charts with the plan as a second line, so a miss is visible without reading numbers. The example slides at the top of this guide come from Brambleroot's deck built on this outline.

### 7. Send the deck as a pre-read

Sending the deck ahead means the meeting starts with questions instead of a readout. **Share the deck on pre-read day**, and ask attendees to read it and leave questions on the slides beforehand. Owners of off-plan metrics answer those comments before the meeting, so the discussion starts from what's still open.

### 8. Spend the meeting on what's off plan

The meeting is for the metrics that missed and the decisions they need. Skip **every metric that's on plan** unless someone asks about it. For a 60-minute MBR, try this split:

- 2 minutes: the decisions needed today
- 5 minutes: last month's actions and projects
- 8 minutes: the scorecard and trends
- 30 minutes: each off-plan reason and its decision
- 10 minutes: risks and asks
- 5 minutes: confirming owners and dates

Opening with the decisions tells everyone what the meeting has to settle before the review starts.

### 9. Log decisions and actions for next month

Every decision becomes an action with one owner and a due date. **Write the actions on the deck's last slide during the meeting**, then send them to attendees the same day. That list becomes the second slide of next month's deck, so the MBR starts where this one ended.

## Common problems

### The meeting turns into a status readout

Reading every metric aloud fills the time before the off-plan metrics come up. Keep on-plan metrics to the scorecard slide, put the off-plan slides right after it, and move anything for information only to the appendix.

### A metric's trend suddenly breaks

A jump with no business reason usually means the definition or source changed. Compare this month's definition with the appendix slide. If the change was deliberate, note it on the trend chart and restate past months with the new definition when you can.

### The same actions roll over every month

Actions drift when nobody owns them or they have no date. Give each action a single named owner and a due date, and put an action that's late twice on the agenda, as in [updating last month's actions](#5-update-last-months-actions).

### The numbers aren't ready on numbers day

A source that closes late holds up the whole deck. Agree numbers day with each metric's owner, and with finance if the books close late. If one number is still missing, send the deck with that metric marked "pending" rather than holding the pre-read.

## Questions

### What is a monthly business review?

A monthly business review (MBR) is a meeting where a team or business unit compares the month's results with its plan and with the month before. It explains the metrics that missed, checks last month's actions, and agrees new actions with owners. It usually runs on a scorecard of 6 to 10 metrics and a short deck sent ahead.

### What is the format of a monthly business review?

An MBR is a short deck sent ahead, then a meeting that follows it. The deck has a scorecard of actual vs plan vs last month and one slide for each reason a metric missed. It also covers last month's actions, risks and asks, and new actions with owners and dates. Definitions and sources go in an appendix, so the numbers can be checked.

### How long should an MBR meeting be?

An MBR usually runs 60 to 90 minutes. Keep at least half of it for the metrics that are off plan and the decisions they need. If the scorecard alone takes half the meeting, cut it to fewer metrics.

### What's the difference between an MBR, a WBR and a QBR?

A [weekly business review](https://www.agent-native.com/articles/weekly-business-review/) (WBR) tracks the same few operating metrics every week to spot what moved. A monthly business review (MBR) compares the month with the plan and corrects course. A quarterly business review (QBR) resets goals for the next quarter. A QBR held with a customer reviews the account's quarter; see [how to make a QBR presentation](https://www.agent-native.com/articles/qbr-presentation/).

### Who should attend an MBR?

An MBR's attendees are the team's leader, the owner of each scorecard metric, and a finance partner who knows the plan. Keep the group to people who own a number or a decision; others can read the deck and the action list instead of attending.
