Check four things: how it handles your own vocabulary, whether it works in the apps you actually type in, what it does to your text after you speak, and whether you can find a dictation again later. Test all four before you switch.

The reason to care is simple. If you dictate prompts, tickets, and messages all day, a tool that mangles your product names or only works in its own window costs you the time you were trying to save.

## What should you look for?

| What to check             | The question to ask                                                      |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Your vocabulary           | Does it learn names, product terms, and library names, or does it guess? |
| Where it works            | Does it type into any text field, or only inside its own window?         |
| What it does to your text | Do you get your raw words, a cleaned-up version, or both?                |
| History                   | Can you search past dictations?                                          |
| Cost and ownership        | Is it free or paid, closed or open source?                               |

## What are your options?

| Your situation                                                                         | Option                                     | What to check                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| You dictate now and then                                                               | Your operating system's built-in dictation | Free and already there. Check how it handles technical terms and whether it works in every app. |
| You dictate all day                                                                    | A dedicated dictation app                  | Check vocabulary support, which apps it works in, and the price.                                |
| You have a finished recording                                                          | A transcription tool                       | That's transcription, not dictation. You speak first and get the text later.                    |
| You want dictation, history, and meeting and screen-recording transcripts in one place | Clips                                      | Needs the desktop app. Check that your operating system is supported.                           |

Agent-Native publishes Clips, so weigh the last row accordingly. Built-in dictation is a good place to start if you only dictate occasionally.

## How does dictation work in Clips?

1. Install the Clips desktop app.
2. Click into any text field, like your agent's prompt box, a ticket, or a message. Hold Fn and speak.
3. Release. Clips keeps the transcript and a cleaned-up version in your dictation history.
4. Search that history later. Clips searches across dictations, recordings, and meetings in one library.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the difference between dictation and transcription?

Dictation turns your speech into text as you speak, usually into the app you're in. Transcription turns a finished recording into text afterward.

### Can dictation software learn technical terms?

Some tools support a personal vocabulary. Clips does. It biases transcription toward the names and terms you add.

### Do I need the desktop app to dictate in Clips?

Yes. Hold Fn in the Clips desktop app to dictate into other apps.

### Are my dictations saved?

Yes. Every dictation is kept in a searchable history, with the transcript and a cleaned-up version of the text.

### Is Clips free?

Yes. Clips is free and open source. It's labeled alpha, so expect rough edges.
