Decide the offer, the audience, and the one action you want first. Then have an AI tool draft the page, compare a couple of versions, and edit before you ship. Design makes interactive HTML prototypes you can share and export. It doesn't publish the page, so deploying is a separate step.

The AI part is the easy part. The decisions you make before you ask for a page are what separate a page that works from a page that looks fine.

## What should you decide before you ask AI for a landing page?

- The offer: what the visitor gets, in one sentence.
- Who it's for, and what they already know.
- The one action you want them to take.
- The proof you have: customers, numbers you can stand behind, or a demo.
- The objection most likely to stop them.

Put those in a short brief. Everything the AI draws or writes should trace back to it.

## What are your options for building one?

| Your situation                                   | Option                                                | What to check                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| You want the page live today                     | A page builder that generates and publishes           | Check how much you can edit, who owns the code, and what you pay.        |
| You want to compare directions first, then build | A prototype tool like Design, then deploy it yourself | The prototype isn't a live page. Deployment and forms are still yours.   |
| You have a repo and an engineer                  | A coding agent building it in your codebase           | You review code instead of a design. Direction changes cost another run. |
| You're short on time and the page is simple      | Start from a template                                 | Fast, but it looks like everyone else's.                                 |

Agent-Native publishes Design, so weigh the second row accordingly. If you need the page live today, a publishing page builder fits better.

## How does it work in Design?

1. Paste the brief: the offer, the audience, and the call to action. Link a design system so the page matches your brand.
2. Ask for a few versions. Design shows them side by side on the canvas.
3. Pin comments to specific elements and send them to the agent, or edit text and styling yourself.
4. Export HTML or a ZIP of the files.

## What should you check before you ship it?

Design doesn't publish pages, so plan the deployment step. HTML exports may use external runtime resources, so open the export in a clean browser before you hand it off. Read every claim on the page against what you can actually prove. Design is labeled alpha, so expect rough edges.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI build a landing page?

Yes. AI tools can draft a page from a brief. Check the copy and the claims, because the draft reflects the brief you give it.

### Does Design publish the page?

No. Design creates interactive HTML prototypes you can share for feedback or export. Publishing and deploying the page is a separate step.

### Can I use my own brand?

Yes. Link a design system to guide colors, typography, styling, and brand instructions. You can reuse it across designs.

### Can I edit the page after AI generates it?

Yes. Adjust text, spacing, and styling with visual controls, or ask the AI agent to change the design.

### Is Design free?

Yes. Design is free and open source. It's labeled alpha, so expect rough edges.
